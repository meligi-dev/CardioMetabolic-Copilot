# ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes — 2024
## Chapter 12: Neuropathy, Retinopathy, and Diabetic Foot Care

### 12.1 Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy (DPN) Screening & Pharmacotherapy
* **Recommendation 12.1:** All patients with diabetes should be screened for diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) at diagnosis of type 2 diabetes and at least annually thereafter using 10-g monofilament testing plus pinprick or vibration sensation. (Level B)
* **Recommendation 12.2 (First-Line Pharmacotherapy for Neuropathic Pain):** Initial pharmacological treatments recommended for symptomatic diabetic peripheral neuropathic pain include:
  1. **Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors (SNRIs):** **Duloxetine** (30–60 mg daily, Class I, Level A).
  2. **Gabapentinoids:** **Pregabalin** (50–150 mg TID, Class I, Level A) or **Gabapentin** (300–1200 mg TID).
  3. **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs):** Amitriptyline or Nortriptyline (25–75 mg at bedtime; use caution in elderly due to anticholinergic and orthostatic risk).
  * *Opioids and Tramadol are NOT recommended* for neuropathic pain management due to dependence risk and lack of long-term efficacy. (Level B)

### 12.2 Diabetic Retinopathy Screening
* **Recommendation 12.3:** Patients with type 2 diabetes should have an initial dilated eye examination by an optometrist or ophthalmologist at the time of diabetes diagnosis, and annually thereafter if retinopathy is present. If eye exams are normal and glycemia is well-controlled, exams every 1–2 years may be considered. (Level B)
