# IDSA & Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2024
## Infectious Disease & Acute Hospital Antibiotic Protocols

### 1. Surviving Sepsis Campaign: 1-Hour Hospital Bundle
* **Recommendation 1.1 (Initial Resuscitation):** For sepsis-induced hypoperfusion or septic shock (lactate ≥4 mmol/L or MAP <65 mmHg despite fluids), administer **at least 30 mL/kg of IV crystalloid fluid** (e.g., Lactated Ringer's) within the first 3 hours of presentation. (Class I, Level A)
* **Recommendation 1.2 (Empiric Antimicrobial Timing):** Administer broad-spectrum IV antimicrobials **within 1 hour of recognition** of septic shock or high suspicion of sepsis. Obtain blood cultures prior to antibiotic administration without causing significant delay. (Class I, Level A)
* **Recommendation 1.3 (Vasopressor First-Line):** **Norepinephrine** is the first-line vasopressor to target a Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) of **≥65 mmHg**. Add Vasopressin (0.03 units/min) rather than escalating norepinephrine to very high doses. (Class I, Level A)

### 2. Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) Inpatient Treatment
* **Recommendation 2.1 (Non-Severe Inpatient CAP):** Standard empiric regimen:
  * **Beta-lactam (Ceftriaxone 1–2g IV daily or Ampicillin/Sulbactam 1.5–3g IV Q6H) + Macrolide (Azithromycin 500mg IV/PO daily)** OR
  * **Respiratory Fluoroquinolone monotherapy (Levofloxacin 750mg daily or Moxifloxacin 400mg daily)**. (Class I, Level A)
* **Recommendation 2.2 (Severe Inpatient CAP / ICU):** Beta-lactam (Ceftriaxone 2g IV daily or Cefotaxime) + Azithromycin (or Levofloxacin). If MRSA or Pseudomonas risk factors present, add Vancomycin and Piperacillin/Tazobactam. (Class I, Level A)

### 3. Acute Pyelonephritis & Complicated UTI
* **Recommendation 3.1:** Empiric IV Ceftriaxone 1g daily or Ciprofloxacin 400mg IV Q12H for 7 days. Adjust based on urine culture and susceptibility. (Class I, Level A)
